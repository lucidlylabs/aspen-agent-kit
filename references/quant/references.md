# References

The papers and books behind the claims in this section. Where a card says an effect is "documented"
or grades an indicator, this is the source.

**Why this list matters:** it is the difference between a strategy built on evidence and one built on
what a chart looked like. Anything here can be found, read, and checked. Prefer sources you can verify
over confident assertions — including the ones in this kit.

## Market microstructure — how prices actually form

- **Kyle, A. (1985).** "Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading." *Econometrica* 53(6), 1315–1335.
  The origin of λ, the price-impact coefficient. Why trading moves prices in proportion to size.
- **Glosten, L. & Milgrom, P. (1985).** "Bid, Ask and Transaction Prices in a Specialist Market with
  Heterogeneously Informed Traders." *Journal of Financial Economics* 14(1), 71–100.
  Why the spread exists: adverse selection. The foundation of market making.
- **Amihud, Y. (2002).** "Illiquidity and Stock Returns: Cross-Section and Time-Series Effects."
  *Journal of Financial Markets* 5(1), 31–56. The ILLIQ measure.
- **Cont, R., Kukanov, A. & Stoikov, S. (2014).** "The Price Impact of Order Book Events."
  *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 12(1), 47–88. Order flow imbalance.
- **Easley, D., López de Prado, M. & O'Hara, M. (2012).** "Flow Toxicity and Liquidity in a
  High-Frequency World." *Review of Financial Studies* 25(5). VPIN.
- **Hasbrouck, J. (2007).** *Empirical Market Microstructure.* Oxford University Press.
- **Bouchaud, J.-P., Bonart, J., Donier, J. & Gould, M. (2018).** *Trades, Quotes and Prices.*
  Cambridge University Press. The modern reference on order books and impact.

## Execution and market making

- **Almgren, R. & Chriss, N. (2000).** "Optimal Execution of Portfolio Transactions."
  *Journal of Risk* 3(2), 5–39. The impact-vs-timing-risk trade-off.
- **Avellaneda, M. & Stoikov, S. (2008).** "High-Frequency Trading in a Limit Order Book."
  *Quantitative Finance* 8(3), 217–224. The standard market-making model: reservation price and
  optimal spread.
- **Ho, T. & Stoll, H. (1981).** "Optimal Dealer Pricing Under Transactions and Return Uncertainty."
  *Journal of Financial Economics* 9(1). Inventory risk.

## Momentum and factor returns

- **Jegadeesh, N. & Titman, S. (1993).** "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers."
  *Journal of Finance* 48(1), 65–91. Cross-sectional momentum.
- **Moskowitz, T., Ooi, Y.H. & Pedersen, L.H. (2012).** "Time Series Momentum."
  *Journal of Financial Economics* 104(2), 228–250. Across 58 instruments and decades. The core
  evidence for trend following.
- **Asness, C., Moskowitz, T. & Pedersen, L.H. (2013).** "Value and Momentum Everywhere."
  *Journal of Finance* 68(3), 929–985.

## Technical analysis — the honest record

Read these together; they tell one story about how an apparent edge survives scrutiny.

- **Brock, W., Lakonishok, J. & LeBaron, B. (1992).** "Simple Technical Trading Rules and the
  Stochastic Properties of Stock Returns." *Journal of Finance* 47(5), 1731–1764. Found significant
  results for MA and range-breakout rules.
- **White, H. (2000).** "A Reality Check for Data Snooping." *Econometrica* 68(5). The method.
- **Sullivan, R., Timmermann, A. & White, H. (1999).** "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule
  Performance, and the Bootstrap." *Journal of Finance* 54(5), 1647–1691. **Applied the correction and
  most of the significance disappeared.** The most important methodological result in this space.
- **Lo, A., Mamaysky, H. & Wang, J. (2000).** "Foundations of Technical Analysis." *Journal of
  Finance* 55(4), 1705–1765. Formalised chart patterns; some convey information.
- **Park, C.-H. & Irwin, S. (2007).** "What Do We Know About the Profitability of Technical Analysis?"
  *Journal of Economic Surveys* 21(4), 786–826. The comprehensive survey.
- **Zakamulin, V. (2017).** *Market Timing with Moving Averages.* Palgrave Macmillan. Shows how many
  popular MA variants are mathematically near-identical.
- **Wilder, J.W. (1978).** *New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems.* The origin of RSI, ATR, ADX and
  Parabolic SAR. Worth reading for the definitions.

## Volatility

- **Mandelbrot, B. (1963).** "The Variation of Certain Speculative Prices." *Journal of Business*
  36(4). Fat tails and volatility clustering.
- **Engle, R. (1982).** ARCH. *Econometrica* 50(4). — **Bollerslev, T. (1986).** GARCH.
  *Journal of Econometrics* 31(3).
- **Parkinson, M. (1980)**; **Garman, M. & Klass, M. (1980)** — both *Journal of Business* 53(1);
  **Rogers, L.C.G. & Satchell, S. (1991)**, *Annals of Applied Probability* 1(4);
  **Yang, D. & Zhang, Q. (2000)**, *Journal of Business* 73(3). The range-based volatility estimators.
- **Andersen, T. & Bollerslev, T. (1998).** "Answering the Skeptics: Yes, Standard Volatility Models Do
  Provide Accurate Forecasts." *International Economic Review* 39(4). Realised volatility.
- **Corsi, F. (2009).** "A Simple Approximate Long-Memory Model of Realized Volatility."
  *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 7(2). HAR-RV.

## Statistical arbitrage

- **Engle, R. & Granger, C. (1987).** "Co-Integration and Error Correction." *Econometrica* 55(2).
  Why cointegration, not correlation, is the right test.
- **Gatev, E., Goetzmann, W. & Rouwenhorst, K.G. (2006).** "Pairs Trading: Performance of a
  Relative-Value Arbitrage Rule." *Review of Financial Studies* 19(3), 797–827.
- **Avellaneda, M. & Lee, J.-H. (2010).** "Statistical Arbitrage in the US Equities Market."
  *Quantitative Finance* 10(7), 761–782.

## Position sizing and capital growth

- **Kelly, J.L. (1956).** "A New Interpretation of Information Rate." *Bell System Technical Journal*
  35(4), 917–926.
- **Thorp, E. (2006).** "The Kelly Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting, and the Stock Market."
- **MacLean, L., Thorp, E. & Ziemba, W. (2011).** *The Kelly Capital Growth Investment Criterion.*
  World Scientific. Includes the case against full Kelly.
- **Grinold, R. & Kahn, R. (1999).** *Active Portfolio Management.* The fundamental law: skill × breadth.

## Backtesting, overfitting and statistics

The most important cluster in this list, and the most ignored.

- **Bailey, D. & López de Prado, M. (2014).** "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias,
  Backtest Overfitting, and Non-Normality." *Journal of Portfolio Management* 40(5).
- **Bailey, D., Borwein, J., López de Prado, M. & Zhu, Q. (2014/2017).** "The Probability of Backtest
  Overfitting." *Journal of Computational Finance.*
- **Harvey, C., Liu, Y. & Zhu, H. (2016).** "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns."
  *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 5–68. Argues for a t-statistic threshold near 3.0.
- **López de Prado, M. (2018).** *Advances in Financial Machine Learning.* Wiley. **The single most
  useful book for anyone building systematic strategies today** — bar construction, fractional
  differentiation, triple-barrier labelling, purged cross-validation, sample weighting.

## Prediction markets and forecasting

- **Thaler, R. & Ziemba, W. (1988).** "Anomalies: Parimutuel Betting Markets: Racetracks and
  Lotteries." *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 2(2), 161–174. Favorite-longshot bias.
- **Wolfers, J. & Zitzewitz, E. (2004).** "Prediction Markets." *Journal of Economic Perspectives*
  18(2), 107–126. The standard survey: accuracy, information aggregation, price-as-probability.
- **Manski, C. (2006).** "Interpreting the Predictions of Prediction Markets." *Economics Letters*
  91(3). Why price is not exactly probability.
- **Snowberg, E. & Wolfers, J. (2010).** "Explaining the Favorite-Longshot Bias: Is It Risk-Love or
  Misperceptions?" *Journal of Political Economy* 118(4).
- **Brier, G. (1950).** "Verification of Forecasts Expressed in Terms of Probability."
  *Monthly Weather Review* 78(1). The scoring rule.
- **Tetlock, P. & Gardner, D. (2015).** *Superforecasting.* On producing calibrated probabilities —
  the actual skill required to trade these markets on forecasting edge.

## Books worth reading end to end

- **López de Prado, M. (2018).** *Advances in Financial Machine Learning.* Start here.
- **Ilmanen, A. (2011).** *Expected Returns.* The best single survey of what drives returns across
  asset classes, including carry as a risk premium.
- **Bouchaud et al. (2018).** *Trades, Quotes and Prices.* For microstructure and execution.
- **Chan, E.** *Quantitative Trading* and *Algorithmic Trading.* Practical, implementation-focused.
- **Narang, R.** *Inside the Black Box.* How systematic funds are actually structured.

## A note on crypto-specific literature

Peer-reviewed work on perp funding, basis and crypto microstructure is thinner and younger than the
equity literature, and quality varies widely. The general microstructure and factor results above
transfer well; the crypto-specific claims in this kit
([indicators/crypto-native.md](indicators/crypto-native.md)) are grounded in venue mechanics and
observable data rather than in academic consensus, and are marked accordingly.

**Verify crypto claims against the venue's own documentation and your own recorded data.** That is the
standard this whole kit is built on: [../perpetuals/README.md](../perpetuals/README.md#official-documentation).
